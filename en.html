Privacy Policy

Last updated 12 October 2026

1. Who provides the app?

The Breeder’s Journal (Oppdretterens Kulldagbok) is developed and provided by Carl Fredrik Lilliedahl Jenzer Bålsrød. For privacy questions, contact carl.fredrik1997@yahoo.com.

2. Information you enter

The app lets you record and organize information related to dog breeding, including kennel name, parent dogs, litters and puppies, health tests, mating, temperature, progesterone, birth, weight, reminders, photos, documents and free-text notes.

Information about animals is generally not personal data. Photos, documents, kennel names and free-text notes may, however, contain information about people. You are responsible for ensuring that information you enter may be processed lawfully.

3. Local storage and iCloud

Records, photos and attachments are stored locally on your device. If you enable iCloud sync, app data is also transferred to The Breeder’s Journal’s private CloudKit storage associated with your Apple Account. Attachments may be stored as separate CloudKit records. File names, file types, file sizes and checksums may be used to manage and verify transfers.

Sync takes place while the app is open, active and connected to the internet. The journal content itself is not sent to a separate server operated by the developer. Apple provides iCloud and CloudKit. See Apple’s Privacy Policy.

From version 1.1, you can also share a journal with people who use a different Apple Account. Sharing uses Apple’s iCloud and CloudKit. The shared journal is stored in the owner’s iCloud and made available to invited participants. Content may also be stored locally on participants’ devices.

From version 1.2, you can create multiple kennels, each with separate records and its own private CloudKit zone in iCloud. You can also open kennels that other people have shared with you. The app keeps information about your kennels and your last selected journal locally on the device. Switching kennels does not merge their records.

Sharing does not require a separate account with the developer. Journal content is not sent to a separate server operated by the developer.

4. Camera, photos and notifications

The camera is used only when you choose to take a photo in the app. Photo Library access is used when you choose an existing image. Local notifications are used for reminders and breeder tips; the app does not send remote push notifications through a developer-operated server. From version 1.2, reminders from your primary kennel may be scheduled even when another kennel is open, as well as from other kennels for which you enable notifications. Notifications may display kennel names and registration details on your lock screen, depending on your device settings. Notification scheduling uses locally available information; updates to other kennels require their new data to be synchronized while the app is active.

Permissions can be changed in iPhone or iPad settings. Features that require a permission may then become unavailable.

5. Sync between devices

When iCloud sync is enabled, changes to records and attachments can be transferred between your devices using the same Apple Account. The app does not sync in the background; it must be open and active.

If multiple devices contain conflicting changes, the app may stop syncing and ask you how to handle the conflict instead of automatically overwriting data.

Sharing with co-breeders – from version 1.1

Sharing is optional. From version 1.2, owners can select “Share your kennel” or “Manage kennels” from the kennel menu on Home. The owner invites participants using Apple’s sharing feature and chooses whether they have read-only access or permission to make changes. Permissions are managed through Apple’s sharing sheet. Recipients accept invitations through Apple’s sharing feature.

Sharing covers the entire selected journal, including records, photos and attachments. It is not limited to a particular litter and does not automatically grant access to other kennels owned by the same person. Participants with write access can add, edit and delete content in the shared journal. Your own kennels and journals that others share with you remain separate in the app.

Content may include personal data, such as names and contact details of puppy buyers or people mentioned in documents and free-text notes. Before sharing, review the content and ensure that you have a lawful basis for sharing other people’s personal data.

Apple processes information needed to manage invitations, participation and access. Names and other account or contact information may be visible in Apple’s invitation and sharing features.

The owner can change participants’ permissions, remove participants or stop sharing. The app checks access when communicating with iCloud. Changes may therefore not take effect immediately on a device that is offline or where the app is not active.

When the app detects that access has been removed, normal viewing and editing of the shared journal are blocked. Previously downloaded content may still exist locally. Revoking access does not guarantee that all previous copies have been deleted.

If write access is removed while a participant has changes that have not been synchronized, those changes may be retained locally to prevent loss of work. This does not give the participant permission to continue changing content in iCloud.

6. Backups

You can manually create a complete .kulldagbok backup. It may contain your records, photos and attachments. The backup is a separate file and you choose where to store it. The file is not encrypted by the app. Store it securely and delete copies you no longer need.

iCloud sync is not the same as a separate .kulldagbok backup.

7. Deleting local data

A participant can leave a shared journal without deleting the owner’s journal in iCloud. The app then removes the local journal record for that share and returns to the participant’s own journal. Downloaded attachment files may remain in the app’s local storage.

“Reset app data” resets the journal records for both personal and shared journals on the current device and turns off synchronization there. The locally stored primary-kennel selection and per-kennel notification preferences are also removed. This does not delete the owner’s content in iCloud or backups and exported files stored elsewhere.

8. Deleting kennels and iCloud data

From version 1.2, you can open “Manage kennels” and select “Delete kennel” for a kennel you own. The app requires two confirmations before asking iCloud to delete the selected journal’s own CloudKit data, including records and synchronized photos and attachments. Once iCloud confirms deletion, that kennel’s local records are removed from the current device. If iCloud does not confirm deletion, local data is retained. Deletion cannot be undone.

If the kennel has been shared with other people, participants may lose access to its iCloud content. You cannot delete a kennel someone else has shared with you; you can leave the collaboration instead. Deleting one owner kennel is not intended to delete your other kennels or another owner’s shared data.

The older “Delete all iCloud data” function under “Settings → iCloud” applies to the original journal’s iCloud data and associated legacy CloudKit records. It is hidden when multiple kennels are registered on the device. It does not delete all separately owned kennels. Unlike “Delete kennel”, this older function retains the local records on the device and disables syncing for that journal.

Other devices may retain previously downloaded content. Deletion in iCloud does not automatically remove separate backups, exported files or screenshots; you must manage those copies yourself. A participant with write access may also delete individual content through normal editing.

9. How long is information stored?

The app does not apply an automatic expiry period to your journal content. Data remains locally and, if enabled, in iCloud until you edit or delete it, reset the app, use the iCloud deletion function, or otherwise remove it.

The developer cannot restore journal content that no longer exists on your devices, in iCloud or in an available backup.

10. TestFlight and feedback

If you use a TestFlight version of the app, Apple processes testing-related information and may make crash data, usage information and submitted feedback available to the developer. Your name and email address may be available depending on how you were invited to the test. Screenshots or text that you submit as feedback may contain information from the app.

Such information is used only for testing, troubleshooting, support and improving the app. Information you send directly to the developer may be retained for as long as necessary to handle the request, document an issue or meet legal obligations, and is deleted when no longer needed. Apple’s processing of TestFlight data is governed by Apple’s own terms and privacy rules. See TestFlight and Privacy.

11. Sharing with third parties

The developer does not sell personal data and does not use your journal content for advertising. Apple processes data when you use iCloud, CloudKit, the App Store or TestFlight in accordance with Apple’s own terms and privacy rules.

12. Your rights

For personal data you send directly to the developer, you may contact the developer to request access, correction or deletion in accordance with applicable privacy law. The developer does not have access to enter your private journal or Apple Account to edit or restore content there.

If you believe your personal data is being processed unlawfully, you may contact the Norwegian Data Protection Authority (Datatilsynet) or another competent supervisory authority.

13. Changes to this Privacy Policy

This policy may be updated when the app’s functionality or data processing changes. The date at the top shows when the policy was last updated.
